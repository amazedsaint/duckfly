Select a separate fixed response lag for each estimator using only the two training movies. Freeze the selected lag before computing held-out metrics. Report the entire correlation/error curve as a diagnostic, but do not replace the frozen choice with a better held-out lag.

The predeclared hypothesis is supported only if both neural readouts select a positive training lag, their frozen-lag correlation is at least0.8 on at least five of six held-out motion movies, and their held-out mean absolute rate error improves by at least25% relative to zero-lag comparison on at least five of six movies. Also require conventional flow to select zero training lag and the two stationary movies to stay below0.05rad/s peak decoded rate after0.2s. Report every criterion separately, including failures. A clean direction score alone cannot validate the timing hypothesis.

Separately assess whether a *fixed-delay approximation* is accurate enough to consider a bounded compensator: require held-out frozen-lag rate MAE at most0.15rad/s on every movie. If it fails, report frequency-dependent phase/amplitude response as unresolved rather than pretending one shifted timestamp repairs the measurement. Report per-pulse response onset and peak timing, including pulses that never reach20% of the commanded magnitude. These pulse diagnostics do not change the selected lag.

The model's explicit40ms processing interval remains separate from effective feature age. A selected80ms history lag means an output available at capture+40ms describes approximately capture−80ms motion; it is not current motion with a more convenient timestamp. This diagnosis does not authorize using future images or silently reducing latency. Any later proprioceptive correction must use measured history available to both estimators and face new physical validation.
